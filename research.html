Aleksandar Trklja

Research

Research interests and ongoing questions.

Corpus linguistics, lexical semantics and language change

In my current research, I examine the emergence and development of linguistic conventions through evolutionary dynamics, including processes of recurrence, variation, and selection. I also explore semantic domains and the distributional relations among lexical items, combining corpus-based analysis with experimental research methods.

Translation, cognition and philosophy

I investigate translation as a fundamental cognitive capacity and its relationship to the architecture of language and mind. This work engages with philosophy of language, philosophy of mind, and cognitive science, including intentionality, cognitive phenomenology, reference, conceptual representation, consciousness, and the conditions for linguistic understanding. It also considers what computational systems and large language models can—and cannot—tell us about translation and cognition.

AI and language technology

This strand of research examines large language models and machine translation, particularly their use in multilingual and specialized discourse and the linguistic properties of generated texts. I use high-performance computing and supercomputing infrastructure, including the LEO5 system at the University of Innsbruck, for fine-tuning language models, multilingual machine translation, and evaluating generated legal texts.

Language and law

I worked with Professor Karen McAuliffe (Department of Law, University of Birmingham) on the European Research Council (ERC)-funded project Law and Language at the European Court of Justice, based at the Universities of Exeter and Birmingham. We also collaborated on EUCLCORP, an ERC Proof of Concept project that developed a multilingual parallel and comparative corpus of EU court judgments.
